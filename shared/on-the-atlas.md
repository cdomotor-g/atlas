# On the Atlas — for every repository with an `atlas.json`

The owner keeps one map of every repository, how they depend on each other and where each is up to: **https://cdomotor-g.github.io/atlas/** (source: `cdomotor-g/atlas`). A repository with an **`atlas.json`** at its root is on it, under that file's `id`, and describes itself there (format: the Atlas's `schema/atlas.schema.json`; every field is explained there). The file is public: nothing private goes in it. The repository's own `CLAUDE.md` says anything particular to it.

- **When you start**, look at this repo's panel on the Atlas, or `atlas.json`: what it takes from other repos (`consumes`), which repos rely on it (*Gives to* in the panel), anything *behind upstream*, and anything waiting on the owner.
- **Forward — you change something another repo relies on** (a path under one of this repo's `provides`, or one another repo's `consumes` entry watches; the Atlas's *Links* view lists them): in the same piece of work, tell each consumer. If that repo is attached to your session, make the matching change there too; otherwise open an issue in it titled `[Standalone] [<Model>/<Effort>] Upstream: <what changed> in <this repo>`, with the commit, what changed and what the consumer has to do. A breaking change always gets one. The consumer's own daily Atlas check flags the drift as well, but only your issue says why.
- **Back — this repo needs another to change**: open an issue there (attach the repo with `add_repo` if your session doesn't have it) and list it under `asks` in `atlas.json` with the issue reference. If you can't file it, list the ask without one so it shows on the map.
- **Catching up**: when you refresh something this repo copies or follows from another (a vendored copy, a generated table, a build, a citation), move that `consumes` entry's pin in the same commit — the file its `since` points at, or its `since` date. A drift issue the Atlas opened closes itself on the next check.
- **When you finish**: if where this repo is up to has changed — what's being done now, what's next, what's blocked (on the owner: `"on": "human"`, or `"computer"` when it needs only their sign-in) — update `status` in `atlas.json`, with today's date in `updated`. Add a `components` / `provides` / `consumes` entry when you add a part another repo uses, or start using another repo's. `.github/workflows/atlas.yml` checks the file on every push that changes it and once a day.
- **Opening an issue**: under its `Recommended:` line, add what you know of how it weighs, on a line of its own — `Atlas triage: severity 4 · due 2026-11-25 · effort 2h · cost $40`: `severity` 1–5 (how bad if it's left: 5 broken, unsafe or losing data; 3 ordinary work; 1 a nice-to-have), a real deadline, a rough size, money it needs. Leave out what you don't know. The Atlas's *Priorities* view ranks every open issue by it and guesses the rest.
- **Blocked?** Record it in `status.blocked`: what is blocked, who it waits on (`on`: `human`, `computer`, `agent`, or another repo's Atlas id — *Who clears what* in the shared instructions), its `kind`, `since`, the tracking `issue`, what it `holds` up (issue refs, Atlas ids, short phrases) and the `options` you can see — the straight fix, plus any workaround, partial step or part an agent could take off the owner's plate — each with who could do it (`by`), rough `effort`, `model` for an agent option, and `recommended: true` on the one you'd pick. Remove the entry when it clears. The Atlas draws these on its map (lines to You, Your computer, Agents, or the repo it waits on) and lists them, most-holding first, on its Blockers view.
